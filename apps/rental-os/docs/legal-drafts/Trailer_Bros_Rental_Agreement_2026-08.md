# Trailer Bros Rental Agreement — Owner Draft and Implementation Guide

**Research date:** August 8, 2026  
**Recommended agreement version:** `TB-RA-2026-08-v1`  
**Status:** Owner draft for operational use and attorney review. The customer-facing agreement begins below. The research and implementation appendix is internal and is not part of the customer contract.

This draft is designed around Arizona law, the current Trailer Bros public agreement, the current Trailer Bros operating rules, and common equipment-rental practices. It is deliberately more precise than the current agreement without trying to solve business risk with an overbroad waiver.

# CUSTOMER-FACING AGREEMENT

## TRAILER BROS LLC RENTAL AGREEMENT

**Agreement Version:** `TB-RA-2026-08-v1`

This Rental Agreement (the **“Agreement”**) is between **Trailer Bros LLC** (**“Trailer Bros,” “we,” “us,” or “Lessor”**), marketed under the Trailer Bros AZ brand, and the renter identified in the Booking Summary (**“Renter”**). The Booking Summary, this Agreement, and the Pickup/Return Condition Record together form the rental contract for the equipment identified in the Booking Summary (**“Equipment”**).

### 1. Booking Summary and Equipment

The Booking Summary must identify, as applicable:

- Renter’s name and verified contact information;
- the Equipment by type, year/make/model if available, VIN and/or plate/unit number;
- scheduled pickup/delivery and return date and time;
- the base rental rate and rental period;
- approved add-ons and separately disclosed charges;
- the Security Deposit;
- delivery address, delivery mileage, and delivery charge if delivery is selected;
- applicable taxes or other legally required charges; and
- the payment channel or marketplace through which the booking was made.

For Trailer Bros’ current 14-foot utility trailer, the published equipment specifications are: **7,000 lb GVWR, approximately 1,800 lb empty weight, and a maximum cargo payload of 5,200 lb.** Renter must never exceed the lowest applicable limit established by the trailer, axles, tires, coupler, hitch, tow vehicle, or applicable law. The maximum cargo payload is not permission to exceed the tow vehicle’s ratings, gross combination weight rating, hitch rating, tongue-weight limits, tire limits, or any other applicable limit.

### 2. Rental Term; Possession; No Automatic Extension

Renter’s right to possess and use the Equipment begins when Trailer Bros releases the Equipment to Renter or Renter’s approved driver and ends at the scheduled return time shown in the Booking Summary, unless Trailer Bros approves an extension.

An extension is valid only after Trailer Bros approves it in an attributable written or electronic record identifying the new return time and any additional charge. A request for an extension does not itself extend the rental. Renter must keep the Equipment available for return at the agreed time and place.

### 3. Renter and Authorized Drivers

Unless Trailer Bros gives prior written approval, only the Renter may tow or control the Equipment. Each authorized driver must:

1. be at least 25 years old;
2. hold a valid driver license appropriate for the tow vehicle and use;
3. be legally permitted and physically capable of safely towing the Equipment;
4. comply with this Agreement and all applicable laws; and
5. use a tow vehicle and towing components that are suitable and properly rated for the actual load.

Renter remains responsible for compliance with this Agreement by every person to whom Renter gives possession, access, or control, even if that person was separately approved by Trailer Bros.

### 4. Tow Vehicle and Connection Requirements

Before the Equipment moves, Renter must independently confirm that the tow vehicle and towing setup are safe, compatible, and properly rated for the actual combined load. Unless the Booking Summary expressly states otherwise, the current utility trailer requires a properly rated **2-5/16-inch hitch ball**, functional safety chains, a compatible electrical connection, functioning required lights, a properly functioning electric trailer-brake controller, and a properly attached breakaway system.

Renter must inspect the hitch/coupler connection, latch and pin, safety chains, breakaway cable, electrical connector, lights, tires/wheels, jack, gates/ramps, and load before departure and before each later towing operation. If Trailer Bros assists with hookup, loading guidance, or a visual check, Renter must still perform and accept the final towing-safety check before moving the Equipment. This provision does not excuse Trailer Bros from responsibility that applicable law does not permit it to transfer.

### 5. Pickup Inspection and Acceptance

Trailer Bros will provide Renter a reasonable opportunity to inspect the Equipment at handoff and to review or create a Pickup Condition Record, including photographs where available. Renter must either complete/acknowledge the inspection or affirmatively decline the opportunity in the rental system before departure.

Renter must promptly identify any visible damage, missing item, unsafe condition, or material discrepancy. By taking possession after the inspection opportunity, Renter acknowledges receipt of the identified Equipment in the recorded condition, subject to defects or conditions that were not reasonably discoverable on an ordinary inspection.

### 6. Risk of Loss and Custody

To the extent permitted by Arizona law, the parties agree in writing that **risk of loss of or damage to the Equipment transfers to Renter when Renter accepts possession of conforming Equipment and remains with Renter until the Equipment is actually returned to and accepted by Trailer Bros.**

Renter is financially responsible for physical loss or damage occurring during that risk period to the extent provided in this Agreement, including damage caused by collision, theft, vandalism, road hazards, improper towing, improper loading, overloading, unsecured cargo, unauthorized use, or acts or omissions of Renter or persons given access by Renter.

Renter is not responsible under this risk-allocation provision for ordinary wear and tear, damage documented as pre-existing, or loss to the extent caused by a defect existing at handoff or by Trailer Bros’ negligence, willful misconduct, or other responsibility that applicable law does not permit Trailer Bros to transfer.

### 7. Loading and Safe Use

Renter is solely responsible for selecting the cargo, determining its actual weight, distributing the load, maintaining proper tongue weight, securing cargo, and staying within every applicable rating and legal limit. Renter must use appropriate tie-down points and securement equipment for the particular load.

Renter must not:

- overload the Equipment or tow it with an inadequately rated or unsafe vehicle, hitch, ball, receiver, brake controller, electrical system, tire, or other towing component;
- carry passengers on or in the Equipment while it is moving;
- use the Equipment while impaired by alcohol, drugs, fatigue, or any condition that makes towing unsafe;
- use the Equipment for an illegal purpose, racing, speed testing, intentional abuse, or in a manner contrary to manufacturer limitations;
- sublease, rent, lend, transfer, pledge, sell, abandon, or otherwise give away possession or control except to an approved driver under this Agreement;
- alter, dismantle, paint, drill, weld, modify, or make non-emergency repairs to the Equipment without Trailer Bros’ prior approval;
- transport explosives, unlawful materials, hazardous waste, or regulated hazardous materials for which the Renter, vehicle, driver, or Equipment lacks required authorization;
- transport livestock unless Trailer Bros expressly approves that use in advance; or
- take the Equipment outside the United States under any circumstances.

Normal use on maintained public roads and reasonable maintained access surfaces is permitted. Renter must not use the Equipment on terrain, trails, or surfaces that are unsafe for the Equipment or reasonably likely to cause damage.

### 8. Interstate Travel

The Equipment may leave Arizona only with **Trailer Bros’ prior written approval for that specific rental**. Approval may include route, destination, insurance, duration, or other conditions. Travel to Mexico, Canada, or any other location outside the United States is prohibited.

### 9. Ongoing Safety Checks; Defects and Breakdown

Before each tow, Renter must check the connection, safety chains, breakaway system, lights, visible tire/wheel condition, gates/ramps, cargo securement, and any condition that could make continued use unsafe.

If a warning, defect, unusual handling condition, tire/wheel problem, brake issue, lighting failure, structural concern, or other unsafe condition appears, **Renter must stop using the Equipment as soon as it is safe to do so and promptly contact Trailer Bros.** Renter must not continue towing equipment Renter knows or reasonably should know is unsafe.

Renter must obtain Trailer Bros’ approval before arranging a non-emergency repair. Renter is responsible for roadside, towing, repair, or recovery expense to the extent the event resulted from Renter’s misuse, collision, overloading, improper towing/loading, road hazard, prohibited use, or other responsibility allocated to Renter under this Agreement. Trailer Bros is responsible to the extent an expense results from a pre-existing mechanical defect or other Trailer Bros responsibility under applicable law. Emergency actions reasonably necessary to protect people or property are not prohibited by this section.

### 10. Collision, Theft, Vandalism, or Other Loss

Renter must promptly notify Trailer Bros of any collision, theft, vandalism, fire, impound, seizure, significant damage, or other material loss involving the Equipment. Renter must also contact law enforcement when required by law or reasonably appropriate for theft, vandalism, collision, or suspected criminal conduct.

Renter must protect the Equipment from further damage when it is safe to do so, preserve reasonably available evidence, obtain identifying and insurance information from involved third parties when appropriate, and reasonably cooperate with Trailer Bros and applicable insurers in investigating the event. Renter may not abandon the Equipment.

### 11. Rental Charges and Taxes

Renter will pay the charges disclosed in the Booking Summary and any additional amount properly due under this Agreement. Taxes and mandatory governmental charges will be collected or treated as included in the quoted price as stated in the Booking Summary and as required by applicable law.

Advance direct bookings may be processed through an approved payment provider. Same-day direct rentals may be paid using a payment method Trailer Bros accepts for that transaction. Marketplace bookings may be processed through the marketplace’s payment system. Nothing in this Agreement represents that Trailer Bros has a card or other payment method “on file” unless the applicable provider actually supports it and Renter has separately authorized its use.

If Renter has separately authorized a payment provider to store or charge a payment method, Trailer Bros may submit properly documented amounts due under this Agreement to that provider to the extent the authorization and applicable law allow. Otherwise, Trailer Bros may invoice Renter. Trailer Bros will provide reasonable notice or documentation of post-return damage or other variable charges.

### 12. Delivery

If Renter selects delivery, the delivery charge is calculated at **$2.50 per one-way road mile** from Trailer Bros' private configured service origin to the delivery address. The routing provider's road distance is **rounded up to the next whole mile before multiplication**. For example, 3.4 road miles is billed as 4 miles, for a $10.00 delivery charge. The resulting delivery charge must appear in the Booking Summary before Renter accepts the booking.

Trailer Bros may use a commercially reasonable routing provider to determine road mileage. The private service-origin address need not be displayed to Renter. If a route cannot be calculated reliably or the address/route is ambiguous, Trailer Bros will review the delivery quote rather than apply a fabricated default mileage. A manual override must be reflected in the Booking Summary before acceptance or in a later accepted rental record.

Delivery does not transfer responsibility for cargo loading, towing suitability, or safe use except to the extent expressly stated in the Booking Summary or required by applicable law.

### 13. Security Deposit

The Security Deposit shown in the Booking Summary secures Renter’s obligations. It is **not** a cap on Renter’s responsibility and is **not** a cancellation fee.

After return, Trailer Bros will inspect the Equipment. If there is no properly chargeable damage, missing property, excessive cleaning, unpaid rental charge, or other amount due, Trailer Bros will initiate return or release of the Security Deposit within 48 hours after the completed return inspection, subject to the payment provider’s processing time.

If Trailer Bros proposes a deduction, it will provide a reasonable itemization identifying the condition or charge and the amount or reasonable estimate. Trailer Bros may retain from the Security Deposit only amounts properly due under this Agreement and must return the undisputed balance. Returning the deposit does not waive a later claim for latent damage that could not reasonably have been discovered during the ordinary return inspection, but Trailer Bros bears the burden of reasonably connecting any later claim to the rental period.

### 14. Cancellation and No-Show

Trailer Bros removes confirmed reservations from availability and may turn away other renters in reliance on a booking.

**Cancellation 48 hours or more before the scheduled rental start:** Renter receives a full refund of amounts paid for the canceled rental.

**Cancellation less than 48 hours before the scheduled rental start, or no-show:** Trailer Bros may charge a **Late Cancellation Charge equal to the lesser of (a) $100 or (b) the scheduled base rental charge for the canceled booking.** The parties intend this charge as a reasonable advance estimate of the short-notice booking and administrative harm that is difficult to determine when the reservation is made, and not as a penalty.

If custody of the Equipment never transfers, the Security Deposit is refunded in full. Unearned delivery charges and unprovided add-ons are also refunded. Any amount paid above the applicable Late Cancellation Charge and any other amount actually earned is refunded.

If a marketplace imposes a mandatory cancellation/refund rule that conflicts with this section for a marketplace booking, the marketplace rule controls only to the extent of that conflict.

### 15. Early Return, Approved Extensions, and Late Return

An early return does not automatically entitle Renter to a prorated refund.

An approved extension is charged at the rate Trailer Bros discloses and Renter accepts when the extension is approved. If Renter keeps the Equipment beyond the scheduled return time without an approved extension, Trailer Bros may charge **$10 for each commenced hour of unapproved delay, capped at the then-applicable daily rental rate for each additional 24-hour period**, plus other actual damages or commercially reasonable recovery expense caused by the default to the extent permitted by law. Trailer Bros will not obtain duplicate recovery for the same loss.

### 16. Return Condition and Return Inspection

Renter must return the Equipment at the agreed location and time, with all rented accessories and components, in substantially the same condition as at handoff except for ordinary wear and tear. Renter must remove cargo and personal property and return the Equipment reasonably clean.

Trailer Bros will create or complete a Return Condition Record, including photographs when reasonably available, before making a final Security Deposit decision. Trailer Bros may perform an urgent repair or take steps necessary to prevent further loss before a dispute is resolved, but will preserve reasonable documentation of the claimed condition when practicable.

Renter should report any disagreement about an itemized post-return charge within five calendar days after receiving it. Failure to respond within that period does not waive a right that applicable law does not permit Renter to waive.

### 17. Damage, Loss, and Amounts Recoverable

Subject to the risk allocation in Section 6, Renter is responsible for the reasonable, non-duplicative loss Trailer Bros sustains because of damage to, loss of, or improper use of the Equipment during Renter’s custody. This may include, when actually applicable:

- reasonable repair parts and labor;
- towing, recovery, storage, or impound expense;
- replacement of missing rented components or accessories;
- reasonable cleaning expense beyond ordinary turnover cleaning;
- damage involving the frame, axles, suspension, wheels/tires, deck, gate/ramp, lights/wiring, brakes, coupler, jack, fenders, safety equipment, or other components;
- actual documented rental revenue lost during a commercially reasonable repair or replacement period to the extent caused by the covered loss;
- objectively supportable diminished value that remains after repair, only to the extent it does not duplicate repair or replacement recovery; and
- actual commercially reasonable third-party or administrative expense incurred because of Renter’s breach or the covered loss, to the extent recoverable under applicable law.

Trailer Bros will credit amounts received for the same loss from the Security Deposit, insurance, salvage, a responsible third party, or another source so that it does not obtain double recovery.

For a total loss or unrecovered theft, the claim will be based on the reasonable cost, at the relevant time and place, of replacing the Equipment with substantially similar equipment, together with other non-duplicative amounts recoverable under this Agreement and applicable law, less applicable salvage or other recoveries. **No fixed contractual “replacement value” operates as either a penalty or an artificial ceiling on actual loss.**

### 18. Insurance

Renter must maintain motor-vehicle liability insurance required by law for the tow vehicle and must determine before departure whether Renter has insurance or other financial protection applicable to towing, rented trailers, physical damage, liability, and the intended use.

Unless the Booking Summary expressly states otherwise, **Trailer Bros does not provide Renter with liability insurance, physical-damage coverage, or a damage waiver, and Trailer Bros does not represent or guarantee that Renter’s personal or commercial policy covers the Equipment.** Renter is responsible for confirming coverage with Renter’s insurer or agent.

The existence or absence of insurance does not expand or reduce either party’s contractual responsibility except to the extent applicable law, an insurance policy, or an insurer’s payment requires otherwise.

### 19. Third-Party Claims and Indemnity

To the extent permitted by law, Renter will indemnify and hold Trailer Bros harmless from third-party claims, damages, liabilities, and reasonable costs **to the extent caused by** Renter’s or an authorized driver’s negligence, willful misconduct, unlawful use, unauthorized use, or material breach of this Agreement.

This indemnity does not require Renter to indemnify Trailer Bros for a claim to the extent caused by Trailer Bros’ own negligence, willful misconduct, or liability that applicable law does not permit Trailer Bros to shift to Renter.

### 20. Default; Recovery of Equipment

Renter is in default if Renter materially breaches this Agreement, including by failing to return the Equipment when the right to possess it has ended, refusing a lawful demand for return, using the Equipment in a prohibited or materially unsafe manner, materially misrepresenting eligibility or identity, or failing to pay an amount properly due.

After default, Trailer Bros may terminate Renter’s right to possession and use remedies available under this Agreement and applicable law. Trailer Bros may recover possession without judicial process **only when and to the extent the recovery can lawfully be accomplished without breach of the peace**. Renter is responsible for actual commercially reasonable recovery expense caused by Renter’s default to the extent permitted by law.

If facts reasonably indicate theft, conversion, fraud, or other criminal conduct, Trailer Bros may report the facts to appropriate law enforcement. A good-faith billing dispute by itself is not characterized by this Agreement as theft.

### 21. Renter Personal Property

Trailer Bros is not responsible for cargo or other personal property Renter leaves in or on the Equipment except to the extent loss is caused by Trailer Bros’ negligence or another responsibility that cannot lawfully be excluded. Trailer Bros may remove and reasonably store abandoned property and may handle it as permitted by applicable law.

### 22. Privacy and Identity Verification

Trailer Bros may verify identity, age, driver-license status, contact information, payment information, and other information reasonably necessary to administer the rental, prevent fraud, comply with law, or protect the Equipment. Trailer Bros will use and retain personal information according to its then-current Privacy Notice and applicable law.

The Rental Agreement itself should record the verification result and only the identity information reasonably needed for the transaction. A driver-license photograph is not incorporated into this Agreement merely because it was used during verification.

### 23. Marketplace Bookings

If Renter books through a third-party rental marketplace, mandatory marketplace terms governing payment processing, marketplace fees, cancellation/refunds, or marketplace-provided protection control only to the extent they conflict with this Agreement. This Agreement otherwise governs custody, authorized use, safety, condition, return, and responsibility for the Equipment to the extent the marketplace permits.

### 24. Notices and Operational Communications

Routine notices, approvals, extensions, condition reports, charge notices, and other rental communications may be delivered to the email address or mobile number associated with the booking, or through the applicable booking platform. Renter must keep that information current during the rental.

Operational contact for Trailer Bros: **trailerbrosaz@gmail.com** and the current Trailer Bros customer-service number displayed in the Booking Summary or rental system.

Nothing in this section changes legal requirements for service of process or a notice for which applicable law requires a different method.

### 25. Governing Law; Disputes; Attorney Fees

Arizona law governs this Agreement without regard to conflict-of-law rules. To the extent a court-selection provision is enforceable, the parties consent to the appropriate state or federal courts located in or serving Maricopa County, Arizona.

Before filing a non-emergency lawsuit, the parties are encouraged to give each other a reasonable written description of the dispute and a reasonable opportunity to resolve it. This does not restrict a party from seeking emergency relief, preserving a limitations period, recovering the Equipment as lawfully permitted, or exercising a right that cannot lawfully be restricted.

Attorney fees and court costs may be awarded as provided by applicable law. This Agreement does not create an automatic one-way attorney-fee obligation against Renter.

### 26. Entire Agreement; Order of Documents; Severability; No Waiver

The Booking Summary, this Agreement, and the Pickup/Return Condition Record are the complete agreement for this rental except for a later attributable written or electronic record accepted by the parties. Advertising, FAQs, prior discussions, or informal statements do not change the contract unless expressly incorporated into an accepted rental record. Mandatory marketplace terms control only as stated in Section 23.

If a court finds a provision unenforceable, that provision will be enforced to the maximum lawful extent or severed as appropriate, and the remaining provisions continue in effect. A party’s delay or failure to enforce a provision once is not a waiver of future enforcement.

Any approved change to dates, rates, authorized use, destination, or other material rental terms should be documented in an attributable written or electronic rental record identifying the change and the parties’ acceptance.

# INTERNAL RESEARCH AND IMPLEMENTATION APPENDIX

**Do not display or incorporate this appendix into the customer contract.** It explains why the customer-facing language changed and what the Rental OS implementation must enforce.

## A. Executive assessment

The current public Trailer Bros agreement has a useful core, but several provisions create avoidable enforceability, consistency, and operational-evidence problems. The revised agreement above keeps the useful pieces—equipment identification, a deposit that is not a liability cap, damage responsibility, Arizona governing law, and late-return economics—while replacing provisions that are unnecessarily punitive, ambiguous, or inconsistent with how Trailer Bros actually takes bookings.

The most important legal architecture is Arizona **UCC Article 2A**, because the business is leasing movable goods. Many consumer rentals also fall within Article 2A’s “consumer lease” definition. That makes the written risk-of-loss allocation, completeness of the rental record, reasonable remedies, and clean acceptance/return evidence more important than adding increasingly broad waivers.

This is a researched owner draft, not an attorney opinion. It is deliberately structured so Arizona rental/transportation counsel can review identifiable decision points instead of rebuilding the document from scratch.

## B. What changed from the current live agreement

| Current/live concept | Recommended revision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Trailer Bros called “Owner” | Use **Lessor / Rental Company** | The LLC can be the contracting lessor without falsely implying the LLC is the titled owner. Separately document the titled owner’s authority for the LLC to rent the asset. |
| 5,800 lb cargo limit | **5,200 lb** max payload, plus lowest-rating rule | Corrects the owner-confirmed spec and prevents the trailer payload number from overriding tow-vehicle/component ratings. |
| Security deposit can function like cancellation money | Separate deposit completely from cancellation | Deposit secures performance/damage; a cancellation charge should stand on its own rationale. |
| Flat $100 inside cancellation period | **Lesser of $100 or scheduled base rent**, inside 48 hours | A $100 forfeiture on a $60 one-day rental has penalty optics. Arizona liquidated-damages law focuses on reasonableness rather than labels. |
| Current site FAQ uses 24 hours | **48 hours everywhere** | One canonical rule prevents consumer-facing contradiction. |
| Delivery zones ($20/$40/$65) | **$2.50 per one-way road mile; miles rounded up first** | Matches current owner rule and produces one auditable formula. |
| “All damage regardless of fault” | Written risk-of-loss period with defined exceptions | Article 2A expressly permits written allocation of risk; this is more precise and defensible. |
| Fixed $4,800 total-loss value | Actual reasonable substantially-similar replacement cost, with credits | A stale fixed number can under-compensate Trailer Bros or act oddly as a ceiling; actual loss is more adaptable. |
| $150 administration fee | Actual commercially reasonable administrative/third-party expense | Reduces punitive/double-recovery risk and maps better to Article 2A remedies. |
| Repair + diminished value + lost income can all stack | Allow each only to extent actual, supported, and non-duplicative | Protects legitimate loss without building a penalty stack. |
| “Repossess without notice” | Recovery as permitted by law; nonjudicial only without breach of peace | Tracks Arizona Article 2A self-help limits. |
| Non-return “may be theft” | Civil default first; law enforcement when facts reasonably support criminal conduct | Removes coercive criminal language from an ordinary payment/return dispute. |
| Broad “any and all” indemnity | Third-party indemnity to extent caused by renter conduct; carve out Trailer Bros fault | Arizona construes indemnity provisions strictly, especially around an indemnitee’s own negligence. |
| Renter insurance is “primary” | Renter must verify coverage; Trailer Bros does not promise coverage exists | Insurance priority is a policy question. The contract should not manufacture coverage or priority. |
| Assumes a stored payment method | Channel-neutral payment authorization | Direct cash, payment processors, and marketplaces do not all work the same way. |
| All off-road/unimproved use prohibited | Prohibit unsafe/damaging terrain; permit reasonable maintained access | More realistic for utility-trailer customers and therefore more enforceable operationally. |
| No real return evidence structure | Pickup + return condition records with photos and affirmative inspection/decline | Evidence prevents more disputes than aggressive prose. |
| ID image can become part of signed artifact | Keep license photo outside agreement; retain verification marker/minimum data | Driver-license data increases privacy/breach exposure and usually need not live in the signed contract. |
| One broad e-sign acknowledgment | Separate e-consent and high-risk acknowledgments; provide downloadable executed copy | Stronger consent, attribution, and electronic-record evidence. |
| No version identifier | Immutable agreement version on contract and booking | Lets Trailer Bros prove the actual terms accepted for each rental. |

## C. Delivery formula to implement exactly

For Trailer Bros delivery:

1. Use the private configured Trailer Bros service origin.
2. Calculate **one-way road distance** from that origin to the customer’s delivery address using the configured mapping/routing provider.
3. Round the road distance **up to the next whole mile**.
4. Multiply the rounded mileage by **$2.50**.
5. Store the raw route distance, rounded billable miles, rate, final fee, routing result/provider, and any authorized override in the booking audit record.
6. If the mapping service fails or the address/route is ambiguous, require owner review; do not invent or silently default a fee.
7. Do not expose the private service-origin address to the customer merely to explain the calculation.

Example: 3.4 road miles → 4 billable miles → **$10.00 delivery charge**.

## D. Rental OS acceptance/evidence requirements

The contract will only be as good as the transaction evidence around it. The OS should make these records immutable or append-only after execution:

- agreement version and exact accepted agreement content or content hash;
- Booking Summary values at signature time;
- separate e-transaction consent;
- separate acceptance of towing/safety, risk-of-loss/damage, cancellation, and travel restrictions;
- typed/signed name and signature artifact;
- verified email or other reasonable attribution signal;
- signature/acceptance timestamps and timezone;
- pickup condition photos and inspection completion or affirmative decline;
- return condition photos and inspection record;
- extensions and other material changes with time, author, new terms, and customer acceptance;
- post-return charge itemization and supporting evidence; and
- signed/executed agreement copy made available to the renter.

Do **not** silently overwrite a signed agreement when the template changes. New template version = new version identifier. Existing executed rentals remain attached to the exact version accepted.

## F. Attorney-review hotspots

These are the issues worth paying Arizona counsel to decide rather than asking counsel to rewrite obvious operational language:

1. **LLC/titled-owner relationship.** The trailer is currently personally titled while Trailer Bros LLC is the contracting lessor. Document the owner-to-LLC right to rent/use the asset, or align title/ownership if appropriate. The customer contract should keep calling the LLC the **Lessor**, not the titled “Owner,” until that is resolved.
2. **Commercial insurance.** Contract language cannot substitute for appropriate business liability/physical-damage coverage. Confirm coverage with a broker that understands peer/direct trailer rental and the actual entity/title arrangement.
3. **Warranty disclaimer.** Arizona Article 2A permits conspicuous disclaimer of implied merchantability/fitness warranties when statutory language is satisfied. I intentionally did **not** put a broad “AS IS / WITH ALL FAULTS” disclaimer in the recommended live text. On a safety-critical consumer trailer, the benefit should be weighed against safety, consumer-fraud, and negligence optics. Have counsel make that call explicitly.
4. **Cancellation economics.** Have counsel bless the lesser-of-$100-or-base-rent formula and its 48-hour window based on actual booking history once Trailer Bros has enough data to show short-notice loss.
5. **Damage/loss-of-use proof.** Confirm the preferred evidence standard and whether any predefined reasonable schedule is worthwhile. The current draft uses actual documented, non-duplicative loss rather than penalty-like flat fees.
6. **Privacy/ID retention.** Adopt a short Privacy Notice and a real retention/deletion schedule. Do not embed driver-license photographs in signed PDFs by default.
7. **Marketplace conflicts.** Compare this contract against Big Rentals / Neighbor’s Trailer or any marketplace actually used, especially damage protection, payment, cancellation, insurance, and off-platform collection restrictions.
8. **Tax.** Confirm Arizona TPT licensing, rate/application, and whether quoted consumer pricing will be tax-inclusive or show tax separately. The rental agreement should merely reflect the correctly configured Booking Summary.

## H. Things deliberately not copied from large rental companies

The research surfaced common commercial terms that would make this agreement look more aggressive without necessarily making Trailer Bros better protected. The recommended draft therefore does **not** currently include:

- a broad release of Trailer Bros’ own negligence;
- mandatory arbitration or a jury-trial waiver;
- a class-action waiver;
- a broad “AS IS / WITH ALL FAULTS” consumer warranty disclaimer;
- a one-way automatic attorney-fee provision;
- a fixed $150 administration charge;
- a stale fixed replacement value;
- an unlimited “loss of use” formula untethered to actual loss;
- repossession language that omits the breach-of-peace limitation; or
- a contractual threat to treat every late/non-return as criminal theft.

Those omissions are intentional. For a small consumer-facing trailer operation, precise risk transfer, proof of condition, accurate public representations, lawful remedies, and insurance are more valuable than stacking provisions that a court may narrow or that create ugly consumer-fraud optics.

## I. Launch blockers that contract language cannot fix

Before treating the revised contract as a substitute for operational risk management, Trailer Bros should close these items:

1. **Confirm appropriate commercial insurance in force.** The agreement cannot manufacture coverage.
2. **Document Trailer Bros LLC’s legal right to rent the personally titled trailer** or align title/entity ownership.
3. **Confirm Arizona TPT licensing/configuration and tax presentation.** A.R.S. § 42-5071 treats tangible-personal-property rental as a TPT classification.
4. **Publish and enforce a short Privacy Notice/data-retention schedule**, especially for driver-license information.
5. **Make the website FAQ, booking flow, agreement, delivery calculator, confirmation messages, and marketplace listings use the same rules.** Inconsistency itself creates avoidable consumer-fraud and evidence problems.

## J. Recommended counsel brief

Instead of asking the next lawyer, “Can you write me a rental agreement?”, send them this draft and ask for a focused Arizona review of:

> “Please review this consumer utility-trailer rental agreement under Arizona UCC Article 2A, Arizona electronic-transactions law, Consumer Fraud Act considerations, indemnity/assumption-of-risk law, trailer safety law, privacy/driver-license handling, TPT treatment, and our entity/title/insurance structure. Please identify any provision you would change for enforceability, and explain the risk you are correcting. We specifically want a view on risk-of-loss transfer, the 48-hour lesser-of-$100-or-base-rent cancellation charge, damage/loss-of-use remedies, self-help recovery, marketplace conflicts, and whether a conspicuous Article 2A warranty disclaimer should or should not be added.”

That should turn the attorney engagement into a targeted review of the remaining 10% rather than paying someone to rediscover the operating facts.
